THE PURPOSE

Why monitor motor performance?

Movement and coordination are important parts of everyday activities. Repeated motor testing provides an opportunity to explore how performance may vary over time.

A single test only gives a snapshot of performance. By repeating the same tasks regularly, this project can investigate patterns across multiple sessions.

THE SCIENCE

Why study Friedreich's ataxia?

Friedreich's ataxia is a rare inherited condition that can affect coordination, balance and movement. This project explores whether repeated digital motor tests could provide useful information about changes in motor performance over time. It is a research investigation, not a clinical study or diagnostic system.

ABOUT THE PROJECT

A Young Scientist research project

MotorMonitor was developed by Sean Toomey as part of the Stripe Young Scientist & Technology Exhibition. The project combines website development, digital testing and research into motor performance.

The aim is to investigate whether simple computer-based tasks, repeated over time, could be a useful way of collecting information about motor performance. The project also considers the challenges of keeping testing consistent and interpreting results responsibly.

NEED TO KNOW?

Frequently asked questions

A few things participants may want to know before using MotorMonitor.

How often should I complete the tests?

The project is designed around weekly testing. Try to complete each session at approximately the same time each week, following the instructions provided by the researcher.

How long does a session take?

The four tests are designed to be short. The exact time can vary depending on how quickly each participant completes them.

Should I use the same device every week?

Yes. Using the same device and keeping the screen orientation consistent can help reduce differences caused by the equipment.

Can I see my previous scores?

Previous scores are not displayed to participants during testing. This helps keep each session focused on the current tasks rather than comparisons with earlier results.

Is MotorMonitor a medical diagnosis tool?

No. MotorMonitor is a student research prototype. It cannot diagnose Friedreich's ataxia or any other medical condition.

What happens to the test results?

Results are stored for the research project and may be analysed to explore patterns across repeated sessions. Participation and data handling should follow the project's consent, privacy and oversight arrangements.

Important research notice

MotorMonitor is a student research prototype and is not a medical diagnostic tool. Test results cannot diagnose Friedreich's ataxia or determine whether someone has a medical condition. Changes in performance can have many possible causes. Participation should be voluntary and follow appropriate consent, privacy and research oversight arrangements.
