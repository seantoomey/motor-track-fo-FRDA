FRIEDREICH'S ATAXIA RESEARCH PROJECT

Understanding movement. One week at a time.

MotorMonitor is a digital motor testing project developed as part of the Stripe Young Scientist & Technology Exhibition. Created by Sean Toomey, it investigates whether short weekly motor tests can help researchers study changes in motor performance associated with Friedreich's ataxia (FRDA).

THE PROCESS

How MotorMonitor works

A straightforward weekly process designed to collect consistent motor performance data over time.

01

Complete the tests

Participants complete four short digital motor and reaction tests during each weekly session.

02

Record the results

Test results are securely recorded for the research project, allowing performance to be examined across multiple sessions.

03

Study changes over time

Researchers can analyse patterns across repeated sessions while considering natural variation and other factors that may affect performance.

THE SCIENCE

Why study Friedreich's ataxia?

Friedreich's ataxia is a rare inherited condition that can affect coordination, balance and movement. This project explores whether repeated digital motor tests could provide useful information about changes in motor performance over time. It is a research investigation, not a clinical study or diagnostic system.

Important research notice

MotorMonitor is a student research prototype and is not a medical diagnostic tool. Test results cannot diagnose Friedreich's ataxia or determine whether someone has a medical condition. Changes in performance can have many possible causes.
